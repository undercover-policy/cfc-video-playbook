# Lessons from building real videos

355 lessons, grouped by topic. Each was learned on a real CFC piece or from published research and is in use.

## Intake

- **Ask for every approval at intake, in one batch.** List every download, install, and license decision the build will need in the first reply, with file, source, size, and license, and ask for all of them at once. A late approval request stalls the whole pipeline.
- **Load the taste profile and rejected list at intake.** Read TASTE.md, including the rejected list, before proposing a concept, palette, figure style, or music. State in the brief which defaults apply and which questions are open.
- **Start from the idea and let Claude ask the intake questions.** Begin every video with the idea only, and have Claude ask for the goal, the audience, the one action the viewer should take, where it will be published, the length, and the format. Decide the answers together and write them down before any storyboard or code.
- **State length and aspect ratio as hard limits.** Give the exact length in seconds and frames at a stated fps (180 s = 5,400 frames at 30 fps) and the aspect ratio, never shorthand like '90s', and verify them after render. Scene ranges must add up to the total.

## The brief

- **Brief parallel scene workers with a written animation guide.** Before splitting scenes across subagents, write an animation guide: the requester's direction verbatim, the code contract with a short example, the canvas and reserved areas, what the engine already draws, the helper API with safe ranges and allowed options, style rules, and exact self-check commands. Put CFC's hard rules in an addendum no prompt can override, and keep the story in the storyboard.
- **End card: one ask, the CFC URL, an optional campaign URL, held 4 s.** End with one ask ('Reach out'), CaliforniaFamily.org, and, when a campaign applies, its URL in the campaign's color. Hold the card 4 s after the last spoken word.
- **Hand Claude the tool and asset inventory.** Tell Claude which tools, licensed assets, reference folders, and prior projects exist, where they live, and which ones are off limits. Without an inventory Claude guesses or improvises a stack.
- **Keep a written profile per entity and read it before every task.** Put the organization's profile (canonical name, URL, phone, address, taglines, audience, tone) and a never-claim list in one file that every task reads first, so nobody retypes a phone number wrong.
- **Name the moment the viewer should feel.** Name the one moment the viewer should feel, the one special effect saved for it, and the exact tone of the last beat. For CFC the ending lands on moral clarity, not ambiguity.
- **Put a perspective block in every brief.** Tell Claude at the start that the script is intentionally one-sided CFC advocacy and that CFC's positions are in the approved script and position file. Claude visualizes them without adding counterarguments, and flags factual problems instead of softening claims.
- **Tell Claude to stop and ask when a tool or key is missing.** Write the fallback into every brief: if a tool, file, or key is missing, stop and ask. Do not let Claude improvise a new stack or widen the scope.
- **Use a block-structured brief.** Write every brief in fixed blocks (inputs, perspective, direction with a Banned list, structure, build contract, facts, limits, plan-first start) and keep the technical header locked while only the story block changes.

## References

- **Approve a rendered style frame before scene work.** Before building scenes, render one still that shows the stage, palette, type, one hero shot per layout recipe, and the safe zones, and get it approved. It is the last cheap point to change the look, and every scene is then held to it.
- **Generic real places from reference photos, viewed and never downloaded.** For a school, gym, finish arch or light tower, look at several reference photos in the browser (nothing downloaded), draw a generic place that is no portrait of a real campus, and size every part from real dimensions (a storey 3.7 m, a window 1.9 m) at the scene's px per metre. Record the proportions in the code comment.
- **Give brand context and references, and say exactly what you like.** Before storyboarding, give Claude the brand kit (logos, fonts, exact hex codes, screenshots, previous work) and two to five references, naming exactly what you like in each: camera movement, animation, pacing, how the subject is introduced. Say references are inspiration, point Claude at a past piece it can open as the quality bar, and keep the script, numbers, and quotes fixed.
- **Keep a render environment of licensed assets on the Mac.** Keep one folder of cleared assets on the render Mac (fonts, logos, approved photos, sample and SFX packs, impulse responses) with a license file per pack. Claude finds and uses whatever is present, so put only cleared material there.
- **Keep other creators' films out of the build context.** Feed Claude your own approved style, but keep other creators' films out of its context, or it reuses their characters, sets, and beats. Study references in a separate step and record only what to borrow.
- **Read the event's invitation, theme, and program.** For a video shown at someone else's event, read the invitation, the event page, and the program before storyboarding. Carry the event theme into the end card if it fits, and note every other video or testimony on the program.

## Storyboard

- **A map with a route that draws on and a date label makes a claim concrete.** When the story reaches a place and a date, show a real map, draw the route on in time with the voice, drop the place name as it is spoken, and set the date as type on the map. Build it from real geography with the names and dates read from facts.
- **Alternate dense and calm, and hold the turn.** Alternate dense passages with calm ones, build pressure toward the turn, then give the turn a held beat of about 3 s of darkness or quiet. Calm is not dead time, so cut filler.
- **Carry the story with a recurring motif and a rhyming end.** Give longer pieces one recurring motif, protagonist, or set that returns with visible escalation, and end on the opening image, changed.
- **Declare a layout recipe per beat and cap the static ones.** Name each beat's layout recipe and motion treatment in the storyboard. No two beats in a row share a recipe, and static cards or side-by-sides fill at most one third of the beats, so the film does not become a slideshow.
- **End on one ask and one URL, held at least 4 s.** End on one ask, one URL, and a deadline where there is one, and hold the end card at least 4 s after the last word. Use a UTM-tagged link so clicks can be measured.
- **Hook in the first 3 seconds with big text.** Put the hook in the first 3 seconds and make its words big on screen, because feeds autoplay muted. The hook must read without sound.
- **Keep a library of proven formats as templates.** Build proven formats as reusable templates (documentary explainer, one-board whiteboard, verse card, hymn story, sacred-space seasonal short, talking-head B-roll, bill status card, silent lobby loop) and start each new piece from the closest one.
- **Keep the visual spec and the motion spec apart.** Write what a frame looks like (surfaces, palette, type, prohibitions) separately from how it moves (camera, entrances, rhythm, transitions). The look column holds no motion words; the motion column holds no colours.
- **Let the subject answer in his own words: a sourced quote over the writing figure.** Near the turn, give the subject of the piece one sourced primary-source quote, set in a handwriting or italic face, appearing word by word as it is read, over an image of the act of writing. Attribute it on screen and keep the quote in the fact bank.
- **Make something happen in every shot.** Give every shot an event (something changes between its first and last frame), one focal action with a clear silhouette, cause before reaction, and a payoff for every setup.
- **Put the hook in the first 3 seconds.** Make something meaningful happen in the first 3 seconds, with big text early for sound-off viewing, and use that moment as the cover frame.
- **Show a death through what is missing, never the event.** When a piece reaches a death or a tragedy, show what is missing (the empty place, the object left behind, the name), never the event itself. Do not hold one shot long or cut to a blank screen while the narration carries it.
- **Time every read, one at a time.** List each thing the viewer must understand as a timed read and give each read its own time; if the reads do not fit, lengthen the shot or cut a read, never squeeze. Reveal one new thing at a time and start a reaction only after its cause is seen.
- **Use documentary grammar for civic explainers.** Build narrated civic explainers from a small kit of documentary scenes: archival stills with slow push-ins, quote cards with attribution, big stat cards with year tags, maps, and timelines. It reads as journalism, not a slide deck.
- **Write a story style as a spine, freedoms and checks, not a timeline.** Save what makes a video work as a short brief: the few things every such story has (a real voice and face, a named case, one visual code, a picture for every claim, an anchor and one ask), what stays free (scenes, metaphor, camera, scene count), and what is checked after a draft (holds, safe zones, sourcing). A recipe of beats and seconds copied from one good video, or a library of card types, pushes every later video toward the same slides.

## Script

- **Approve the script before any animation.** Put most of the effort into the script and get it signed off before voice or animation starts. Code cannot rescue a weak argument.
- **Build slack into live-narration scripts.** For live narration, write about 155 words per 92 seconds, give every block a few seconds of slack, and tell the speaker which sentence to drop if he falls behind.
- **Check for real testimonies before showing a composite.** Before showing a composite character, check the program and campaign for real testimonies. If the composite echoes a real person who will be present, change the narration, and add one line of grace for people who made the other choice.
- **Give history pieces a historian and a slower pace.** Assign a named historian reviewer to any history piece, check the timeline for gaps before recording, and slow the pace. Declare any borrowed tune.
- **Keep narration near 150 words per minute.** Write about 150 spoken words per 60 seconds and at most 225 words for 90 seconds, and never speed a voice past 1.1x. Fast speech hurts comprehension and caption timing.
- **Leave it out rather than guess.** If a fact is uncertain, leave it out and list it in NOTES.md for a person to resolve, and label any illustrative graphic as illustrative. Never invent a quote, number, date, or statistic, and allow easter eggs only when they are real and sourced.
- **Log every hook and never repeat one.** Give every piece an ID and log its opening line; check each new hook against the log before it is used. Without a log, a series drifts into the same openings in slightly different words.
- **Make the recorded voice the clock.** Transcribe the narration with word timings, correct the words against the source's own captions, and time every caption, cut, and sound to the spoken words.
- **Nested cue words resolve in order, and a plain cue word is the stable form across cuts.** Resolve the cue words inside a beat (list items, bubbles) in order from the beat's own cue, and start the next beat's search after the last of them; write cues as plain words (the search runs forward from the previous cue) rather than word#n, because the nth occurrence changes when a cut keeps only some sentences.
- **Offer three hooks that open with a number or a date.** When Claude drafts the script, it first offers three opening lines that start with a number or a date, so the hook stat lands in the first 2 seconds.
- **Open with praise, then the charge, then the question.** For a persuasive explainer about a person or an event, the reference opens with two or three parallel superlatives before naming the subject, then states the accusations in the accusers' own words, then asks the one question the piece will answer. For CFC, a superlative is an 'only' or 'first' claim: use one only as a reviewed fact, or keep the adopted number-or-date hook and move the superlatives to the second beat.
- **Put every on-screen line in the brief word for word.** Write every on-screen line into the brief exactly as approved so Claude places signed-off text instead of writing it; any line Claude improves goes back through sign-off.
- **State the verdict last, in one sentence, after the proof.** Hold the thesis until the proof is on the table: story, the other side, the rebuttals, the rule of judgment, the subject's own words and the social proof come first; then one sentence as a full-frame card; then one line that puts the stake on the viewer; then the brand.
- **Urgency only from real facts.** Any urgency or scarcity in a script comes from a sourced fact (a hearing date, a vote deadline, a real limit), never from an invented one.
- **Use the organization's own words for its services and history.** List a partner's services and history in the partner's own words from its own pages. Never list a service it only refers out, and never claim more than its own copy claims.

## Voice

- **A scratch voice for timing drafts is marked so it can never ship.** Use a local Kokoro voice only to time drafts and sheets before the staff recording exists; name the file narration_scratch.wav, let the build mark release.scratch_voice, and keep a release gate that fails until a real recording replaces it. Never send a script to a vendor that licenses inputs for this.
- **Clean a phone-recorded narration gently.** Treat phone-quality narration with a light chain: high-pass near 90 Hz, gentle denoise, a soft expander on room tone, a cut near 280 Hz, presence near 3 kHz, air above 6.5 kHz, and about 2.5:1 leveling. Never change its timing or pitch.
- **Make the voice the clock.** Approve the script, record the voice, measure every word's time into one timing table, and only then animate. Derive cuts, captions, and cues from that table; a new voice take retimes its scene and every scene after it.
- **Measure signal-to-noise at every voice-processing stage.** Measure the noise floor and signal-to-noise ratio before and after each voice stage. Denoise first with a non-local-means filter, then gate, EQ, and compress, and keep a stage only if the ratio does not fall.
- **Non-local-means denoise beats the spectral denoiser on a phone narration.** On a 52 kbps phone recording with a 21 dB signal-to-noise ratio, ffmpeg's anlmdn (s=6) gained about 6 dB of signal-to-noise; afftdn gained none at any setting tried. Then an expander on the room tone, a cut at 280 Hz, presence at 3 kHz, air above 6.5 kHz and 2.5:1 levelling. Measure signal-to-noise and the 2 to 4 kHz share after every stage.
- **Short function words inside a label lose their stress in the scratch voice.** Kokoro phonemizes with eSpeak NG and has no inline phoneme markup. Unstressed function words ('from', 'as', 'to', 'for', 'in') run into the word before ('New from' heard as 'newfrom'). Check the phonemes with the same package, fix only the spoken text (a comma adds stress and a pause; a respelling such as 'frum' adds stress only), voice two or three takes and let the owner pick by ear. The voice is deterministic.
- **Use real narrators only.** Use the real narrator's recording or live narration. Never synthesize or alter a real person's voice, and never use macOS system voices.
- **With a host file and no --narration, the lavalier WAV is the clock, never the camera track.** When the one command gets a camera file, take source/narration.wav (the lavalier) as the narration whenever it exists and only fall back to the camera's own track; the camera track is late by the sync offset, so type lands early and the Whisper check fails.

## Captions

- **Break captions where a reader would pause.** Choose caption breaks with a cost function: never run across a sentence end, avoid crossing a comma, avoid ending on a weak word ('the', 'of', 'to'), and keep one meaning color per caption.
- **Bring caption words in just ahead of the voice.** Start each word's entrance about 70 ms before it is spoken, rise and scale it in from 90 percent, and let a key word land 10 percent large and settle over about 0.3 s. Exit the old phrase before the next one enters.
- **Burn captions into vertical cuts and ship SRT and VTT.** Burn captions into every vertical cut and also generate SRT and VTT files from the same word timings for upload. Ship a plain transcript too.
- **Caption from the approved words, not raw speech recognition.** Caption exactly what is spoken: take the words from the approved script, take the timing from speech recognition, and hand-check every mismatch against the audio. Show written forms on screen ('5.5', 'AB 1955') while timing follows the spoken words.
- **Chunk captions 2 to 4 words with a whole-script optimizer.** Cut captions into chunks of 2 to 4 words, each on screen at least 0.5 s, with a whole-script optimizer that penalizes breaks across commas, before sentence ends, and on weak words.
- **Color key words by meaning.** Color key caption words by one fixed meaning code: pink for girls, blue for boys, red for danger or the lie, aqua for help, white on dark scenes.
- **Color key words by meaning with a fixed legend.** Give caption key words a small fixed color legend tied to meaning, and keep it the same for the whole piece. Color must add meaning, not decoration.
- **Hold the caption that starts on a cut until the cut.** A caption begins fading in 0.17 s before its first word. If a hard cut falls on that word, the caption appears for a few frames at the wrong height or over the wrong shot. Give the phrase that starts on a cut an alpha ramp from the cut time, on both sides of every cut, as a rule and not per case.
- **Let caption color follow the background through crossfades.** Compute caption color from the background tone at every frame, not once per phrase, and strengthen the halo through mid-tone transitions. Text must never sit dark on dark or white on light.
- **Make caption color follow the background every frame.** Compute caption ink from the background tone at every frame, with a halo that strengthens through mid-tones. Never pick one color per phrase.
- **Offer full-sentence subtitles as a per-project caption style.** Word-timed captions of 2 to 4 words (TC-6) stay the default, but a project may choose sentence subtitles at intake when its style and content call for them: one script sentence per subtitle in a burned-in band, at most two lines, never two at once, on screen for the whole of its sentence. SRT and VTT are still written.
- **Record which speech engine timed the captions.** Record the speech-recognition engine, model, and settings that produced each word-timing file, so caption timing can be reproduced.
- **Show 2 to 4 caption words at a time, each at least 0.5 s.** Chunk captions into 2 to 4 words, keep each chunk on screen at least 0.5 s, time it within 80 ms of the voice, and keep each SRT cue line at 84 characters or fewer.

## Type

- **A quote the narrator does not read lands as one block.** When the narrator says other words while a sourced quote shows, a word-timed quote finishes on an arbitrary spoken word and can leave its attribution too little time to read. Land it as one block on its cue word (by_word = false), and test the timing on a natural-pace scratch voice, not only the script check's 150 wpm model.
- **A title holds long enough to read before anything competes with it.** A short title line ('Bodies do.') needs about 1.35 s fully on screen; the next text element (a name super) starts only after it has gone, never during its fade.
- **Capture text crisply: sRGB profile, scale 1, no LCD text.** Launch the capture browser with a forced sRGB color profile, LCD text off, font hinting off, and device scale factor 1. Never put CSS will-change on anything that scales, or the text blurs.
- **Check text along its path, not only at rest.** Models lay text out for its final position, so text collides with other elements while it moves. Check overlaps during entrances and exits too.
- **Cut text that restates what the viewer already sees or hears.** Keep word-timed captions, but cut labels, signs, and callouts that repeat what the picture or voice already says. Allow a bill-number card, one short sourced phrase per scene, and the end card.
- **Display type lands on the spoken word, one accent per card, lines sized into a block.** Key type outside captions should land on its spoken word (phrases word by word, lists line by line), hold 2.5 to 9 s, carry one accent colour per card, and size each line so the block reads as a rectangle. Drive it from the word file, never from typed seconds.
- **Fix punctuation and block em dashes before the first render.** Convert straight quotes and apostrophes to curly ones in every viewer-facing string, and fail the build on any em dash, before the first full render.
- **Give each font one job.** Use one display family for all type and a second family with exactly one job, such as a serif italic only for Scripture.
- **Hold on-screen text at least 2.5 s and keep it under about 8 words.** Keep non-caption text visible at least 2.5 s and at most about 8 words at once (labels 3 words or fewer), with one big number or sentence plus one label per scene. Hold simple cards 5 to 7 s and complex diagrams 8 to 14 s.
- **Hold on-screen text at least 2.5 seconds.** Hold every label and on-screen text at least 2.5 s. If the narration moves on sooner, let the caption carry it and log the exception.
- **In paper styles, text arrives as an object and never fades.** In a paper or print style, text enters physically: typed on, slid in on a strip, stamped, or revealed by an underline swipe. It never fades in, because paper does not fade.
- **Load every font before the page reports ready.** Await document.fonts.load for every face and weight, then document.fonts.ready, before setting the flag the renderer waits for. Confirm each face with document.fonts.check.
- **Load every font face before the page reports ready.** Load each font face explicitly and wait for document.fonts.ready before the renderer captures a single frame.
- **One short label per beat, only for a date, a name, or a number.** Allow one label of 1 to 4 words per beat, on a strip or a stamp, and only when the beat carries a date, a name, or a number. Otherwise the picture and the captions carry it.

## Layout

- **A name super only while the person is on screen.** Do not float a presenter's name and title over animation when she is not in the frame; it reads as clutter and confuses who is speaking. Her own footage carries her title.
- **A style frame stops the figure left of the desk, with the prop's foot on the desk top.** Place every prop by its anchor and compute the desk top from the prop's own geometry before placing anything on it; a figure that walks in stops short of the furniture, never in front of it. The first style frame had the speaker standing in the chair and the document floating 55 px above the desk; both were fixed by anchors and one derived line (deskTop).
- **Check every scene for collisions and edge cuts.** Render a contact sheet for every scene and look for text over busy background, figures cut by the frame edge, and motion that enters the caption band. Fix each before building the next scene.
- **Cut labels that do not earn their place.** Remove decorative text (signs, building names, corner tags) unless the story needs it. Extra text clips, competes with captions, and adds facts to defend.
- **Draw props at the figure's scale: a desk top near .4 of the figure, a seat near .26.** Furniture placed by its anchor still needs its size set against the figure: a desk whose top sits at knee height (.28 h) reads as a coffee table, a chair whose seat is at .19 h as a plinth. Scale the prop so the desk top lands near .4 of the figure height and a seat near .26, and give an upright document a stand.
- **Give big text a quiet background.** Reserve the caption band in exact pixels in every scene brief and keep faces and key action out of it. Where text is big, keep the background behind it quiet, and reuse 2 or 3 fixed text anchors so text never jumps.
- **Keep key content inside platform safe zones.** On vertical social video, keep key content above y 1500 and left of x 960. For projection, keep text at least 110 px from the edges and tell the operator 'fit, not fill'.
- **Keep key content out of the platform overlay zones.** In a 1080x1920 frame keep key text out of the top ~220 px, the bottom ~380 to 400 px, and the right ~140 px, and overlay these zones on review sheets.
- **Keep the host off centre and leave a third of the frame for type.** When a host is on camera, frame them left or right of centre on a flat single-colour wall and leave the other part empty, so type can sit on the wall itself with no box behind it. A name super is a lower third shown once, and only if R-026 is amended for recordings made for compositing (TC-2 bans standing corner labels).
- **Key every layout off the caption band.** Place titles and key graphics relative to CAP.y (captions sit in the upper third on vertical and the lower third on horizontal) and check a contact sheet for collisions; automatic checks miss overlaps.
- **On tall frames, stack timelines top to bottom.** A left-to-right timeline wastes a 9:16 frame: four steps end up small and crowded with alternating labels. On tall frames list the steps top to bottom (a dot and line on the left, the date big, the label under it); keep the horizontal timeline for wide frames.
- **One hero element at about 70 percent of the visual weight.** Give each shot one dominant element (about 70 percent of the visual weight), two or three supporting elements at most, and generous empty space.
- **Tall frames need their own type layout, not the 16:9 one scaled.** On a 9:16 frame the bottom 420 px and the right 120 px of the lower half are the platform's controls. A scene label sits at .76 of the height (not .93), a claim card is narrower (.74 of the width) and higher (.36), and long hero lines wrap. Check with textcheck rules 1 and 4 on the vertical cut before any review; the 16:9 layout is never a crop (D-044).
- **Type never crosses a figure of the scene: the label moves, and the text check sees figures.** A scene label under the set at .93 of the height (or .76 on a tall frame) crossed the seated figure's legs on 16:9 and its head on 9:16, and the text check could not see it because figures logged no box. Now every figure logs its box (scene.js figureBox), the label tries its band, the top band and then the free side, and textcheck rule 7 fails any text through a figure. Give label_x / label_y / label_align in the spec to place a label by hand.
- **Use the worst-case vertical safe zone across TikTok, Reels, and Shorts.** At 1080x1920 keep critical content inside top 96, bottom 340, left 24, and right 132 px, and aim for the comfortable zone: top 168, bottom 496, left 72, right 176.

## Colour

- **Dither gradients with static grain; never animate grain.** Add a faint static grain to dither gradients so the encoder does not band them; never animate it. Build textures once at setup and let no scene add its own.
- **Keep every colour, font and type constant in one theme file.** Put the palette, the house colours, the meaning accents, the dark-field accents, the scene fields, the fonts and the type constants in brand/theme.json; the page builder writes it into build/theme.js before lib.js loads, project.py takes the font defaults from it, and lint.py --engine fails a colour literal in engine drawing code. Move values, never change them, so the golden frames prove nothing moved.
- **One wall colour and one accent for the whole piece.** Give a narrated explainer one background colour for every host and type frame and one accent colour used for one word or line per card, so emphasis is unmistakable and the piece reads as one voice. Scene illustrations may carry their own palettes.
- **Reserve each accent colour and measure its share of the frame.** Keep the signal colour for strokes, underlines, arrows, numbers, and emphasis only, about one red element per scene. Measure the share of each frame covered by each accent, so 'aqua sparingly' and 'red means danger' become checks instead of hopes.
- **Route dark-to-light crossfades through a hue, never gray.** Never crossfade straight between a dark and a light scene; the midpoint turns muddy gray. Route the background through a color (navy to blue to white, or navy to wine to gold to white).
- **Use the host organization's brand for the host's story.** When a video tells a host organization's story and ends in its ask, build it in that organization's colors, fonts, and design language, taken from its current site and logo files.
- **White-forward CFC palette with red and aqua as meaning accents.** Default CFC scenes to white or daylight. Use CFC red for danger and the lie, aqua only where it means help or protection, navy for adults and text, and gold in support. Keep one or two dark scenes for contrast.

## Motion

- **A draw-on for filled art: outline first, then the fill floods up inside it.** In a flat filled style, draw a prop on in two beats: log every filled and stroked shape on an offscreen layer, draw each outline along its edge (nearest the ground first), then flood each fill upward inside its outline and fade the outlines. Keep drop shadows in the mask. Use it only for things that arrive inside a shot, and check the narration leaves the window: here only 0.9 to 1.0 s was free.
- **A figure walks in and stops like a person, on his word.** For a figure who enters and squares up: a short walk (0.76 s here, 1.9 steps a second) that arrives on the word that names him, arms swinging against the legs, the pelvis shifting over the standing leg with the shoulders countering it, a bob highest at mid-stride, the stride fading over the last 0.22 s, then one soft knee dip and a nod. Derive footstep sounds from the same timing.
- **A flash must hand over to the light, never back to the dark.** A two-frame white flash cut back to a dark frame reads as flicker (light, dark, light). Fade the flash out over about 0.4 s while the background under it eases quickly toward the light it is announcing.
- **A group on a slope stops one gap behind the figure ahead.** When several figures climb a path together, give each one a stopping point one gap behind the one ahead (clamp its progress to 1 minus its index times the gap), or the leader stops at the top and the rest bunch into it, which breaks FC-4. A crowd helper needs the same rule in one dimension: a slot at least .42 of the figure height wide, else refuse.
- **A listener's nod must be at least 15 degrees, and a point must end on its object.** On a 440 px figure a 6 degree nod over 0.6 s is invisible on a contact sheet and at full size: use about 15 degrees as a double dip over 0.8 s. A level pointing arm whose line runs to another person's head reads as scolding; aim the point down so the finger ends on the object (the seal), and keep the listener's chin level. A figure that starts inside the frame mid-stride reads as walking across, not in: start off-frame at a natural stride (about 0.6 of the figure height per second).
- **A phrase card over a short sentence lands as one block (by_word = false), or the 2.5 s hold fails.** With by_word on (the default), each on-screen word lands on the next spoken word, so a 7-word card over a 6-word sentence lands its last word on the next sentence and the beat ends 0.6 s later; the hold rule (L-055) stops the build. For a card whose words are more than about half the spoken words of its sentence, set by_word = false: the block lands on the cue and holds the whole sentence. List items with their own cue words need at least six spoken words (about 2.5 s) after the last item's cue.
- **Decorations leave with their parent.** An accent line, badge, or rule fades out with the text it belongs to, before the next element lands in its space.
- **Give a draw-on room in the voice, but not so much the narrator waits.** Doubling every building's draw time with long voice pauses made the narrator sound stuck waiting for the drawings. The kept balance: draws at 1.5 times (about 2 s a building, 3.5 s for the Capitol), voice slowed to 0.87 with 0.5 to 0.8 s pauses, each drawing finished 0.5 to 0.8 s before its word. Mark the pauses in the script for the human recording.
- **Give figures weight: anticipation, settle, and staggered holds.** Start actions with anticipation and a drive, end arrivals with an ease-out and a settle, and give idle figures staggered weight shifts that hold between moves. Never use constant wiggle.
- **Keep overshoot tiny and never bouncy.** Use springs and easing curves, never linear for UI-style motion, and allow a tiny overshoot at most. Bouncy easing is banned.
- **Keep the camera alive, and make big moves mean something.** Give most shots a slow push or drift and save big moves (pull back as scale grows, rise to reveal, fly through into the next scene) for meaning. Change shot size when a scene feels static, zoom in log space, and add a decaying shake only on impacts.
- **Keep visual metaphors dignified, not literal.** Show a metaphor as a physical, dignified action. Do not act out a phrase word for word.
- **Land a figure softly: anticipation, an eased descent, the knees take the weight.** A figure that arrives from above reads as a slam when it falls in a few frames with an accelerating ease. Give it a short rise first, a descent of about half a second that slows into the surface, and a knee bend that straightens over a third of a second; move anyone displaced aside with a step, not a knock.
- **Line art draws on like a pen: outlines first, rising from the ground.** For line art, log every stroked path on an offscreen canvas and reveal the finished drawing through a mask: heavy outlines first, bottom to top, then mid lines, fine lines, hairlines; fills and glows fade in only after the lines around them. A soft gold point on each moving outline reads as a pen. p = 1 must equal the drawing with no draw-on.
- **Make every frame a pure function of time.** Compute every pixel from t inside seek(t): no timers, no CSS transitions, no state carried between frames, and seeded randomness only. The same t must always paint the same frame.
- **Move the whole set as one layer on a camera tilt.** Move the whole set as one layer on a camera tilt or pan. Per-layer parallax on a tilt makes back layers lag and pop out.
- **Move the whole set in a camera tilt; parallax needs a moving camera.** A pan or tilt from a fixed camera has no parallax, so move the whole set as one piece. Use layered parallax only when the camera itself travels.
- **Move, then hold; no constant wiggle.** Give motion a start and an end and let things hold between moves. Idle life (a weight shift, a breath) comes every few seconds, staggered, never as a constant wobble.
- **Place hard cuts between motion-blur sample windows.** When motion blur averages sub-frames, place each hard cut so no frame's sub-samples straddle it.
- **Put hard cuts between frames when blur is on.** With a multi-subframe shutter, place a hard cut so no frame's subframes straddle it, or the frame carries a ghost of the old shot.
- **Render motion blur from subframes.** Render motion blur by averaging several subframes per output frame across a shutter. It makes fast moves read as motion instead of strobing.
- **Secondary motion in a pure-function-of-time renderer is a time offset, not state.** Cloth and hair that lag the body must not keep state between frames (every frame is seek(t)). Sample the figure's own motion function two to four frames back, take a damped average of the velocity over those frames, and turn it into a swing angle. The lag and the damping fall out of the time offset and the weights.
- **Squash a landing about the ground contact, and make it at least 12 percent to read.** Weight on a landing is a squash (wider, shorter) scaled about the point of contact, never about the hip. A 5 percent squash is invisible on a 235 px figure; 14 to 16 percent reads at a glance and recovers in about 0.3 s with a small rebound.
- **Stagger and offset so nothing moves in lockstep.** Give each limb, figure, and group member its own timing offset, phase, and seed, and stagger group entrances by small fixed steps.
- **Start slides just off-frame: spring overshoot scales with the travel.** A spring overshoots by a share of the distance it travels. Start a slide just outside the frame (not a fixed long distance) and damp it (about .75), so paper settles with a few px of bounce.
- **The host is the only still thing: the picture changes about every five seconds.** Keep the picture moving: a scene change, a new line of type, a camera push or a puppet gesture about every 5 s, and let only a live host hold still, except for the planned hush before the key moment (MP-3) and never as constant wiggle (LP-7). Report stillness per segment in the critic's pass.
- **The whip's ghost copies must reach a solid picture at the end of the move.** In a blur whip, make the first ghost copy fully opaque as the move settles (and fully opaque at the start of the smear-out); with uniform ghost alphas the frame after the move averages a translucent picture under motion blur and never reaches the final colour.
- **Tie a walking figure's speed to its stride, or the planted foot skates.** A walk cycle moves the stance foot about .38 of the figure height per step; the body must travel the same distance or the foot slides (a treadmill shuffle that a motion critic measures in one frame). Use strideSpeed(h, cycles, k) for the figure's speed, plant the feet (plant: 1) so nothing hovers through the stride, ease the halt over the last step (walkDistance), and mind the bob's sign (+ is down in drawSide).

## Figures

- **Build figures on a reusable rig with a pose library.** Draw characters from one skeleton rig with named poses and pose blending, so every scene reuses the same figure and new motion is a new pose, not new drawing code.
- **Draw people as shaped silhouettes with no faces.** Draw every person as a shaped silhouette: neck, chest, waist and hips, tapered limbs, hands, shoes, a hair cap, and a kit or trousers. Keep no faces.
- **Export the code-drawn figure as a kit so an illustrator starts from CFC's proportions.** The rig can write itself out as SVG (one named layer per part, a kit.json with the joints) that the kit loader reads straight back; an illustrator redraws inside those layers and the result rides every pose. Two traps: the manifest's height is the figure's own height, not the viewBox (or every limb scales short), and SVG arc caps need sweep flag 0 to bulge outward like the canvas arcs (or every limb loses a half-disc at each end).
- **Faced cartoon likenesses of real historical people.** The reference draws a face on every character, including the real historical subject, and lets the drawn subject speak his own quote. Rejected for CFC: no synthetic likeness of a real person, living or historical.
- **Keep figure groups readable.** When figures stand in front of another figure, draw them in story order and space them so no figure covers another's silhouette.
- **Lock designs and key poses on still sheets before animating.** Approve character, prop, and logo designs, and each shot's key storytelling poses, as still sheets before adding motion. Motion cannot rescue poses that do not read.
- **Map a kit's parts to the rig by joint pairs, and keep the kit's own proportions.** An SVG kit character rides the engine's poses when every part names the two joints it spans (shoulder to elbow, hip to knee, neck to head) in the kit's own coordinates; the loader derives the rig's proportions from those joints and maps each part's native segment onto the posed segment with one translate, rotate and uniform scale. The kit then takes every pose key, the weight keys and the secondary motion with no change to the specs. The segment that places the head must start at the shoulder (neck = shoulder) or the head scales differently from the body.
- **Open-licence icons become house props through one re-style pass, not by hand.** Build an icon kit with tools/kit_from_icons.py: wrap each icon in one group in the house ink, drop every stroke, per-shape fill, invisible artboard rect and opacity, keep the viewBox, write kit.json with one prop per icon and the licence text beside it, then kit_install.py records it. Phosphor fill icons carry fill=currentColor on the root and some an artboard rect; Material Symbols use a viewBox that starts at -960; both pass the loader unchanged after the pass.
- **Plant feet on the drawn ground and drive strides from distance.** Compute foot height from the same function that draws the ground, drive the walk phase from distance traveled so feet never slide, and check each contact with a close-up crop.
- **Shape figures as silhouettes, not stick figures.** Give pictogram figures shaped silhouettes on the skeleton (neck, chest, waist, tapered limbs, hands, shoes, hair cap, kit) in shades derived from the palette.
- **Use anticipation and follow-through in figure motion.** Before a big move, show the load (a set position, a knee bend, arms swinging back); after it, let hair, limbs, and props follow through and settle last. Save squash and stretch and hit-stops for the big beats.
- **Walk cycles need sway, counter-roll, and a settle.** Walks need hip sway over the standing leg, arms swinging against the legs, a shoulder counter-roll, the body highest at mid-stride, a stride that fades out, and a settle at the end.
- **With a face on, the hair cap behind the head must take the face's hair colour.** The silhouette rig draws a slightly larger cap behind the head in the figure's back tone; with a skin-toned head that cap reads as a dark helmet rim. Give the cap the face's hair colour as soon as a face is on.

## Transitions

- **A collapse into an object matches its place, size, shape and moment.** When many figures collapse into the next scene's object, pack them into that object's shape at its exact screen position and size, finish the pack before the object appears, and let the object fade in solid over the pack ahead of its scene's fade.
- **Change the whole picture with a push, wipe or blur whip, and vary the kind.** In a narrated explainer, change scenes with short directional moves (push, slide-in, organic wipe, blur whip, zoom-through, iris, a carried-over element) of 5 to 50 frames rather than hard cuts, and never run the same kind twice in a row. Keep hard cuts for real footage.
- **Cut on a word instead of a short double exposure.** Cut hard on a spoken word when two scenes share no match. A short cross-dissolve between unrelated scenes reads as a double exposure.
- **Cut on the beat or on a word.** Place hard cuts on a musical beat or a spoken word so the edit feels intended.
- **Grow transitions out of the content.** Give every seam a transition that grows out of something on screen (a pin irises open, a field collapses into a ball, a camera flies through). Allow a plain cut only on action or as a deliberate smash cut, and switch scenes only under full cover.
- **Hand every cut over through a moving object; never repeat a transition twice running.** Each transition passes through something already in the outgoing shot (a match, push, iris, or morph), its edge belongs to the outgoing style, and the same transition never runs twice in a row. Generic wipes read as filler.
- **Hand the eye to the same element across a cut.** Across a cut or handoff, keep the viewer's point of focus on the same or a matching element in the same place and size.
- **In hard-cut beats, pieces hold until the cut.** When beats meet in a hard cut, keep every piece fully visible until the cut; a fade-out just before the cut flashes an empty frame (a blink). Let the next beat's pieces arrive on the clean frame instead.
- **Replace double exposures with a clean cut on a word.** When two busy scenes overlap in a short dissolve, cut cleanly on a spoken word instead. A double exposure reads as a mistake.
- **Route dark-to-light crossfades through a hue.** Never cross-dissolve straight between a dark and a light scene, because the midpoint is muddy gray. Route the background through a color ramp (navy, wine, gold, white) or cut on a word.

## Backgrounds and buildings

- **Add restrained, local, text-free background detail.** Give backgrounds California-specific, text-free detail at low contrast so they add depth without competing with the figures or captions.
- **Add restrained, text-free background detail.** Give empty sets quiet, text-free detail drawn at 7 to 20 percent ink, and keep the frame white-forward so the story stays in front.
- **Draw place-true silhouettes with seeded variety.** Draw scenery that fits the place (coast live oaks, eucalyptus, and fan palms for California) with seeded variety in size and mirroring, not generic circle clusters.
- **Draw real places from open map data.** Build place maps from real OpenStreetMap streets and building outlines, show the openstreetmap.org/copyright credit, and send no email address in any map request.
- **Fine lines vanish on a phone: weight line art for the smallest screen.** Line art drawn at desktop weights loses its hairlines on a phone. Scale line weights by class: outlines 1.45 times, mid lines 1.25, fine lines 1.1, on top of a 1.2 base, and check a phone-size still before approval.
- **Give every prop a size and an anchor, and test flatness by counting colours.** A prop pack is usable from a storyboard only when each prop carries its size, its aspect and the point that stands on the ground, so a scene places it by its feet. Prove the flat style by counting the colours that cover 0.4 percent or more of the drawn pixels: a flat prop has 2 to 5 and they cover over 90 percent; a gradient or a texture breaks both numbers.
- **Paint backgrounds past the frame and build depth with overlap and color.** Paint background planes well past the frame edges so pans, zooms, and shakes never expose an edge, and build depth with overlap, scale, and mixing distant layers toward the sky color.
- **Put every scene on one persistent stage.** Describe and draw one stage (a surface, a light, a camera) that every scene lives on, and move elements on and off it. Unrelated backgrounds per scene are the main reason scene 9 stops looking like scene 1.
- **Use motivated light only; no decorative glow.** Allow light only when something in the scene makes it, and derive every light effect from one defined source so light and shadow always agree. Drop decorative glows.

## Charts and maps

- **A chart beat shows a bar rising and lands the number as the fact's own text.** Draw charts from facts only: a bar or line rises on its cue word and the numeral lands once the rise is over, as the fact's on-screen text at its final value, so no false intermediate number is ever on screen (R-018). Votes come from one tally fact per chamber (ayes to noes); one accent per card, by meaning. A fact whose text holds digits passes the number rule (a bill number would), so the storyboard reviewer still reads every chart.
- **A map names only what the story names.** Remove orientation labels (big cities, the ocean) from a story map; label only the places the narration names, as pins.
- **Decode the vendored TopoJSON in a few lines instead of loading a library for one outline.** The US atlas in engine/vendor is TopoJSON in geographic coordinates: arcs are delta-encoded and quantized by a transform. Twelve lines decode a state's rings; an Albers projection with California's own parallels (34 and 40.5) fits it to a canvas. No d3 or topojson-client is needed for a single outline, so a prop can draw the state anywhere the atlas data is loaded.
- **Draw maps from real geographic data and credit it.** Draw maps and scenes from real data (boundaries, terrain, the sun position for the date) with a suitable projection, state any exaggeration, and add any attribution the data license requires.
- **Make every stat label true on its own.** Write every on-screen number with its subject, unit, and basis, so a screenshot of that frame alone is true.
- **Never show false intermediate states.** Do not count numbers up through false values, and do not wipe key words on letter by letter. Fade or scale in the final value or the whole word.
- **Never show false mid-count numbers.** Do not animate a count-up that shows numbers that were never true. Fade or slide in the final value instead.
- **Read on-screen numbers from FACTS, never type them.** Put every on-screen number, date, name, quote, and verse in FACTS with its source, date, a short quote, and a reviewer field, and have scenes read values from FACTS with a data date on screen. Draft charts with placeholders until FACTS supplies the value.

## Music

- **Avoid the synthesis and assembly traps that cause clicks and crashes.** Start every envelope release from the level the attack actually reached, never delay a channel with np.roll (it wraps the tail to the front), and clip every slice to a common length before adding. Scan the decoded audio for clicks.
- **Brief the score with a cue sheet tied to spoken words.** Write a music brief with the tempo, key plan, and a cue sheet that lists each hit, riser, drop, and lift by time and by the word it lands on, plus hard rules from past feedback.
- **Build the score on a beat grid anchored to the first hit.** Pick a tempo, anchor bar 1 on the video's first big hit, and place chord changes and hits on that grid. Export the tempo and offset from the composer so scenes can place beats as B(n) = offset + n x 60/BPM instead of typed decimals.
- **Carve a pocket for the voice in the music.** Dip the music stem gently (2 to 3 dB) around 1.5 to 4 kHz so the voice sits on top without heavy ducking.
- **Do not ship a fully synthesized melodic score.** Take melody from a real recording or from real sampled instruments. Keep synthesis to pads, air, bells, and the ambient layer.
- **Fit the music's breath and lift to the key moment.** Score every candidate start offset from the loudness curve: steady before the key moment, a dip into it, a lift after it, a phrase start at 0:00, and a rest under the end card. Then fine-tune the offset so the lift lands on the frame.
- **Give shrinking-step loops a floor and a cap.** Give any loop whose step shrinks by a factor a minimum step and an iteration cap.
- **Humanize code-composed music with seeded timing and touch.** Offset each note by a few milliseconds and vary its velocity slightly with a fixed random seed, so the score sounds played but stays reproducible.
- **Iterate the soundtrack as its own pass, with variants.** Treat the score as a separate pass: make two or three variants, compare them side by side, and let the approver choose by ear.
- **Keep code scores bright: centroid 700 to 1500 Hz.** Measure the score's power-weighted spectral centroid and aim for 700 to 1500 Hz with real content to 8 to 10 kHz and sub cut below 30 Hz. Code scores tend to come out muffled.
- **Keep strings, synthetic voices, and complex classical out of beds under narration.** Under a voice, avoid string quartets, solo strings, and string orchestras. Never use synthesized voices, choirs, or formant pads. Avoid busy classical pieces and tunes the audience will recognize.
- **Keep synthesized parts bright, not muddy.** Measure the power-weighted spectral centroid of every synthesized stem. Aim for 700 to 1500 Hz, with real content up to 8 to 10 kHz and the sub 15 to 20 dB under the pads.
- **Let the music come to rest under the end card.** Resolve the music and let it rest in the last seconds: about -42 LUFS short-term under a live-narration end card, or one final chord ringing out on social.
- **No synthesized voices, choirs, or formant pads in the music.** Keep vocal-like sounds out of the score: no synthesized voices, formant 'choir' pads, or singing textures.
- **Prefer real instrument samples to pure synthesis.** Where the license allows, sequence real instrument samples (a General MIDI soundfont, a cleared sample pack) instead of pure synthesis; they sound less artificial.
- **Release envelopes from the level reached; never wrap delays.** Release every envelope from the level it actually reached and never use a circular shift for a delay line; both cause clicks and wrapped tails.
- **Render your own performance; do not sample old recordings.** Perform public-domain works yourself: convert ABC, LilyPond, or MusicXML to MIDI and render it through a cleared soundfont or synth. Never use downloaded recordings or unlicensed engravings; only recordings first published in 1925 or earlier are public domain in 2026.
- **Score only with code-composed or public-domain music.** Use only music CFC composes in code or public-domain scores CFC arranges itself. Keep a music notes file that says how every cue was made.
- **Screen candidate recordings before fitting them.** Before fitting a recording, measure its share of energy in the 1 to 3 kHz speech band, scan for applause, check its bandwidth, and confirm its metadata. Dip about 3 dB at 2.5 kHz in any bed under a voice.
- **Start every bed from a real piano recording plus a light ambient layer.** Start each CFC music bed from a simple, emotive solo-piano recording under CC BY or public domain, fitted so its own rest lands on the key moment, with a harmony-following ambient layer under it. Keep a silent version as the fallback.

## Sound effects

- **A no-music version needs a softer effects set, not the same one.** Effects mixed to sit under a score sound loud and electronic once the score is gone. For the no-music master drop the noise whooshes and the reverse swell, roll the hits off above 2.2 kHz (2-pole low-pass), keep the soft stingers, and set the stem 5 dB down.
- **Add subtle, motivated foley.** Add subtle, story-motivated sound effects synced to motion: footsteps by surface, ball hits, room tone. For pressure scenes, use synthesized whispers with no words. Duck all of it under the voice.
- **Compute sound events from the picture's own motion.** Generate the SFX event list from the same motion functions that draw the picture (a footstep when a run phase crosses a landing mark, a hit at the impact time), with screen x for panning and a level per event.
- **Derive sound events from the picture's own motion.** Compute every footstep and impact time from the scenes' own motion exports, and re-export the events after any motion change, so the sounds stay on the feet.
- **Give foley surfaces, alternating feet, and room tone.** Alternate left and right steps slightly in pitch and pan, give each surface its own texture, run interior sounds through a room impulse response, and lay a quiet room tone under interior scenes.
- **Map a drum kit by probing every key.** An orchestral kit's key layout is undocumented; play each key alone and record level, ring time and spectral centroid. The bass drum is the low, long, 60 Hz key (36 here); the crash the bright, three-second, 9 kHz key (66).
- **Put big hits on motion peaks and key words.** Land the biggest hits where the picture peaks and a key word lands, and keep the rest quieter so the peaks read.
- **Re-export sound events after any retime.** Whenever a motion is renamed or retimed, re-export the sound events and rebuild the foley stem, or footsteps drift off the feet.
- **Synthesize UI and transition SFX in code.** Make whooshes, clicks, risers, and impacts in code (noise and sine envelopes) so every sound is owned and reproducible.
- **Use licensed sound banks only inside the mix; never ship the files.** Use a licensed sound bank only as mixed into the soundtrack and never distribute the raw files. Do not use macOS 'say' voices in public or nonprofit work.

## The mix

- **A tight voice over a bed that rests under the end card.** Master the voice tightly (a loudness range near 4 LU), keep the bed a few dB under it, and let the bed go silent under the final card after a last sting; a credit card is where the bed plays alone.
- **Audio mix refactors must be sample-identical.** Run the old and new mix scripts on the same committed stems and require identical samples before replacing the mixer.
- **Automate the score under key lines.** Step the score back a few dB under the most important lines and let it bloom after the last word.
- **Check true peak after the AAC encode.** Measure loudness and true peak on the final encoded file, not only on the WAV, because AAC encoding raises peaks.
- **Duck the music with a speech gate.** Drive ducking from a speech envelope with hold and lookahead (about 220 ms hold, 90 ms lookahead, 60 ms attack, 450 ms release) and drop the music about 6 dB and the foley about 3 dB while the voice speaks.
- **Fail the mix if the bed ever covers the voice.** Duck the bed under speech with a look-ahead gate, and fail the build if any speech window has the bed within 3 dB of the voice.
- **Keep music about 10 to 12 dB under the voice.** Hold the music bed about 10 dB under the voice while someone speaks, and report the voice-to-bed ratio so no window has the bed louder than the voice.
- **Master to -16 LUFS integrated and -1 dBTP.** Master the final mix to -16 LUFS integrated with a -1 dBTP true-peak ceiling, using a two-pass linear loudness normalization.
- **Parse the last ebur128 loudness value.** Read integrated loudness from the last 'I:' line of ffmpeg's ebur128 output, never the first. Check that a mastered score has a loudness range above 2 LU.
- **Parse the last ebur128 summary, not the first match.** When reading ffmpeg ebur128 output, take the last integrated-loudness match; it prints per-frame values before the summary.
- **Use loudness targets by use: -16 LUFS social, -27 LUFS live bed.** Master social video to -16 LUFS integrated and -1 dBTP, with the bed about 12 dB under the voice. Master a bed under live narration to -27 LUFS, and always ship a silent version with it.

## Render

- **A presenter's own footage goes into the frame sequence untouched.** When a real person's recording shares the narration's clock, decode its frames losslessly (BT.709 tv range to RGB PNG) straight into the final frame folder before the canvas render; the renderer skips frames that exist, so nothing of ours (vignette, grain, captions, resampling, JPEG) touches them. Verify with a pixel compare against a fresh decode.
- **A transition paints the leaving picture itself; the leaving beat stays out of those frames.** When a transition draws the old picture into its own layer, suppress the old beat's own draw for the frames the move covers, or the picture is painted twice: the text log then sees the old words twice and reports false overlaps, and a translucent move ghosts the frame.
- **Avoid shell and pipeline traps.** Quote globs in zsh, run scripts from the project root with paths resolved from the script's own file, give every parallel job its own temp name, and pass numeric arguments as separate words.
- **Block and list every request a render does not serve from disk.** During capture, abort every request that the engine's own disk server does not answer, list each one, and fail the render if the list is not empty. No frame may depend on the network.
- **Capture frames with headless Chromium through seek(t).** Render with a headless browser that calls seek(t) for each frame and saves a PNG, then encode with ffmpeg. Serve files through a local route so the page loads nothing from the internet.
- **Check free memory before a render and stop below a floor.** Before a render or any other heavy step, read free memory. Below a floor, stop and print the reading instead of starting a render that stalls the Mac. Run one heavy job at a time.
- **Check tool versions at intake.** Check tool versions and modules at intake and use current flags. This Mac's ffmpeg dropped -vsync (use -fps_mode); Whisper exists only as a command-line tool; librosa and soundfile are not installed.
- **Draft small, and batch the fixes when a change re-renders everything.** Draft at 720p with the layout scaled, so the final keeps the draft's line breaks and its contact sheet matches scene for scene. When a pipeline has no per-scene reuse, a one-word fix costs a full render: collect fixes and re-render once.
- **Encode H.264 High, yuv420p, BT.709-tagged, faststart.** Encode masters as H.264 High profile, yuv420p, with BT.709 matrix, primaries, and transfer tags and faststart. Convert RGB to YUV with the BT.709 matrix explicitly.
- **Encode HD with the BT.709 matrix and tags.** Convert RGB frames to H.264 with the BT.709 matrix and write BT.709 tags. Verify with ffprobe and by decoding one frame against its source PNG.
- **Fail fast on page errors.** Attach page-error and console-error handlers before loading the page, and fail on the first error during boot instead of waiting out the ready timeout.
- **Fail the render on page errors.** Treat any page error or console error during capture as a failed render, not a log line.
- **Fine texture under a moving camera: coarse dots, fixed to the object.** Halftone dots, paper grain, and hairlines are the first detail lost to scaling and H.264 compression, and under a drifting camera they shimmer. Keep dot pitch at least 6 px at 1080 wide, attach texture to the object (not the screen), and check a platform re-encode before delivery.
- **Floor step sizes and cap iterations in search loops.** In any loop that shrinks its step, floor the step and cap the iteration count so it always ends.
- **Halftone breaks up small type: print documents plain, draw their words crisp.** A halftone turns type under about 60 px into unreadable dots. Halftone photos only; print documents and line art plain, and draw their words as real text on top so they stay legible and textcheck can see them.
- **Make every frame a pure function of time.** Draw every frame from seek(t) with no CSS transitions, requestAnimationFrame, clock reads, or unseeded randomness. The same t must give the same pixels.
- **Make renders resumable, and clear frames before a final.** Make renders resumable, write each frame to a temporary file and rename it when complete, and re-render any truncated frame. Clear the frames of an edited shot, or the whole folder before a final, so no stale frame survives.
- **Motion blur and hard cuts apply to live footage too.** A cut into or out of a presenter's footage must also sit between the frame shutter windows ((k + .5) / 30) or the blur averaging blends her with the animation for one frame. Cut a frame or two before a spoken word into her, and on the word boundary out of her.
- **Motion blur never samples across a hard cut.** Sub-frames for motion blur must stay on the frame's own side of a hard cut. Otherwise one frame at every cut blends the two shots (a one-frame double exposure).
- **Multiply globalAlpha, never assign it.** In canvas code, multiply ctx.globalAlpha by a child's alpha; never assign it, or children ignore their scene's fade.
- **Multiply globalAlpha; never assign it.** In canvas code, multiply ctx.globalAlpha by an element's opacity. Never assign it, or the element ignores its scene's fade.
- **One file per scene on a shared engine, registered by time.** Build each scene as its own file on a shared engine, register it with its time range in one registry, and draw a visible placeholder wherever no scene covers the time.
- **Prove order independence: render frames forward and reversed.** Render a sample of frames in order in one page and in reverse across several pages; they must match exactly. Hidden state (caches, lazy setup, ctx.filter on the GPU) shows up as a mismatch.
- **Render a few stills per scene before moving on.** After building each scene, render 3 or 4 stills, including one mid-transition, and look at them. Fix layout, overlaps, legibility, and pacing before the next scene.
- **Review a low-resolution preview before the full render.** Build, then render and review a low-resolution preview before any full-resolution render. Local renders are heavy.
- **Size render workers to the machine.** Size parallel render workers to the machine (8 on the M1 Pro, 2 to 4 on a MacBook Air, 2 on 8 GB), let each pull the next frame from a shared queue, and log milliseconds per frame and an ETA. Keep laptops awake with caffeinate and keep 10 to 20 GB of disk free.
- **Start every final render from an empty frames folder.** Delete the frames folder, or use a new one, before any final render. The renderer skips frames that already exist, so a 'final' can silently ship old frames.
- **Use static grain only.** Use a static dither texture to prevent gradient banding. Never animate grain.

## Checks

- **A checker rule that blocks an approved change gets a narrow exception.** When a rule blocks a change the owner asked for (an inch value in brackets pushed an approved sentence over 25 words), add a narrow, named exception to the checker (an inch value after a mm value is the same measurement) rather than rewording the approved sentence or weakening the rule.
- **A draw-on must log every primitive the drawing uses.** The draw-on reveals only what its instrumented context logged. A primitive it does not log (fillRect, strokeRect, rect, arcTo, fillText, drawImage) pops in at the end instead of drawing on; a drop shadow left out of the mask arrives late. When a drawing uses a new primitive, add it to drawon.js and check a mid-progress still.
- **A hint meant for every build must sit on every build path.** When a measure is printed as a hint for every finished file, put it where every build path passes, not only in the pipeline being worked on. The unchanged-frames share lived only in the one-command narrated-explainer pipeline; a spec build (make_video.py --final) never printed it. explainercheck.py --hint prints the one line and writes nothing; make_video.frozen_hint prints it after every final build, so both paths read the same number.
- **A page error at load gives blank frames while render.py exits 0.** Fail a render when the page logs an error during load or seek: Chromium keeps painting the clear colour, so a broken script produces a whole video of empty frames and every exit code is 0.
- **A test file nobody registers in run_all.py never runs.** Register every tools/test_*.py in tests/run_all.py the day it is written. The suite now fails, naming the file, when a test file on disk did not run.
- **A test that compares zero files must fail.** Every comparison test asserts that it found things to compare. In zsh, pass lists as arrays (unquoted variables do not word-split).
- **After the first full cut, rebuild the weakest scenes.** After the first full version, list and rebuild the two weakest scenes, polish second by second, re-pull frames with the safe zones overlaid, and have a person listen on headphones and a phone speaker.
- **An async seek must be awaited by every caller, including the text log.** If seek() becomes async (preload hooks for large per-frame images), every page.evaluate that reads state after it must await it, or the state is read before the frame is painted and the check passes on an empty log.
- **Automate the rules audit.** Audit every delivery against the research rules and the locked decisions, item by item, and fix or log each one. Automate every item a machine can check.
- **Caption outputs are byte-identical after refactors.** Rebuild words.js, SRT, VTT, script, and transcript from the same inputs and compare bytes before accepting a captions change.
- **Check a drawn face's style on its pixels: colours, gloss and a rim test tell flat from photo.** A flat face has few colours that together cover nearly all of it, no near-white pixels inside, and no dark ring at the head's edge over a light inside. Measure those three on the real render (faceStyleCheck) and plant a texture, a gloss dot and an outline to prove each fails. Hair and beards are dark on both sides of the rim band, so the rim test compares each rim pixel with the pixel just inside it.
- **Check contact sheets, strips, and spot frames.** Review labeled grids of stills across each range, strips around every transition, and close-up crops of figures and contacts, and open each image to look at it. Claude cannot watch video; it can only read frames.
- **Check that the audio stream and the video stream end together.** At finalize, compare the audio stream's own length with the video stream's, within one frame. The file's total length is not enough.
- **Close every piece with a rule-by-rule audit.** Finish with a written audit that walks every locked decision and research rule and marks each PASS, FIXED, or exception with a reason.
- **Fail the build on any Scripture mismatch.** Compare every on-screen verse with the source text of the named translation and fail the build on any mismatch, including the translation label.
- **Gate delivery on scripted pass/fail checks, and never weaken one.** Run a finalize script with pass/fail checks on size, frame rate, frame count, duration, color tags, pixel format, audio format, loudness, true peak, and Scripture. Never weaken a check to make it pass.
- **Have a named Scripture reviewer approve verses and theological claims.** Name a pastor or theologian as Scripture reviewer and have them approve every verse, translation label, and theological claim before release.
- **Keep a footage-usage registry so no source span is used twice.** Record every span of source footage the edit uses (file, in, out) in one registry, and refuse a new window that overlaps a used span unless the storyboard marks it as an intended callback.
- **Keep every footage window clear of the cuts inside its source.** Before placing a window of real footage, find every cut inside the source film with a detector that also sees cuts between shots of the same tone, and keep each window's start and end at least 3 frames clear of them. Otherwise the source's next shot flashes up for a few frames.
- **Let the page enumerate what it can draw, and look at a figure sheet after every change.** A contact sheet of every pose, kit, hand state, face and prop at two sizes, labelled, is the fastest way to see a figure change. Have the page list its own POSE, KITS, PROPS_LOOK and expression tables so a new item appears on the next sheet with no edit to the tool, and read the sheet before writing a test.
- **Look a word up by text both the script and the spoken text share.** When a pronunciation table respells a word for the voice, any scene that finds a word's time by its written form fails. Key a lookup to a word that is spelled the same in both texts.
- **Look at every second of the finished file.** Decode the finished file with the BT.709 matrix and look at a frame for every second, plus strips around every transition, before delivery.
- **Metrics are hints: never tune a film to its checks.** Automated checks catch defects; they do not make a film good. Never add motion just to beat a stillness detector, and always pair the numbers with a look at real frames against the storyboard.
- **Prove a gate works by planting a known-bad item.** Test every check with a deliberately bad input (a false claim, a wrong digit, an unsigned fact) and confirm it fails for the right reason before trusting it on real work.
- **QC every piece against the profile, digit for digit, with a reason for every fail.** Before anything ships, check every script line, caption, on-screen line, and end card against the profile and the fact bank: names, URLs, phone numbers, bill numbers, and addresses exactly, plus never-claims and tone. Report pass or fail with the reason for each fail, and review only the failures.
- **Re-run the text check after moving a piece into the shared library.** Turning a one-off drawing into a shared, parameterized piece changes its layout math. Re-render the frames that use it and run textcheck before and after the move.
- **Recent facts need a fresh human check before release.** Mark facts that change over time (status, counts, titles, records, 'only' and 'first' claims, anything from the last year) as volatile. A named person re-checks each volatile fact in the week of release; neither a model critic nor the model's own memory can clear one.
- **Refactor only behind a golden-frame test.** Before changing engine code, render a fixed set of frames (including motion-blur frames and every scene) from a frozen snapshot and from the new code, and require pixel-identical output.
- **Render a still at every beat's cue word and look at it before building the next beat.** After each beat, render one frame at its cue word and one half a second later into review/stills/, look at them, and give the critic the same frames. Contact sheets show the whole piece; the beat stills show the moment the type lands, where overlaps and weak heroes hide.
- **Run a claim-extraction pass against the sources.** Have two people check every claim on screen and in the voice against primary sources, show the source on screen and in the post, and recheck bill status the week the piece ships. Ask Claude for its source list to confirm the script is not one article in disguise.
- **Run a separate critic with a scoring rubric.** Have a separate critic (a fresh agent) score the render against a written rubric and send it back until every area passes. The builder should not grade its own work.
- **Run an adversarial review before shipping partner-facing work.** Before the final render of anything that carries another organization's name, run two independent reviewers: correctness against the organization's site, social media, filings, and emails, and blast radius on the day. Check each objection against the source and label it BLOCKER, WARN, or REFUTED.
- **Run an adversarial two-reviewer pre-ship review.** Before anything ships, have two independent reviewers try to refute it (correctness and blast radius). Their blocking items stop the render.
- **Run parallel critics with different lenses on the real render.** Give the rendered film to several independent reviewers at once, each with one lens (story and message, motion and layout, trust and facts). One reviewer misses what a panel catches.
- **Save before and after sheets for every change.** Before changing an item, render a sheet of its time range; after the change, render the same times. Keep both images next to the log entry.
- **Scan the finished render for short shots and repeated shots.** After the final render, find every cut in the master and list each shot's length and a fingerprint of its frames. Flag any shot shorter than the project's minimum (2 s in his documentary) and any shot that repeats an earlier one, unless the storyboard allows it.
- **Segment a reference video by transition runs, not cuts, and read 14-frame strips.** To study a video that changes scenes with animated transitions, detect runs of 3 or more high-change frames (two measures merged) instead of hard cuts, then render a labelled strip of about 14 frames across each run so a reader can name the transition and its direction.
- **Test an SVG parser against a twin drawn with plain canvas calls, not against itself.** To prove an SVG loader, draw the same shapes with explicit canvas calls (the same rounded-rect cubics, ellipse, polygon and path numbers) under the same joint transforms and compare pixels: zero differing pixels proves the path parser, the transform stack, the fill handling and the placement at once. A golden frame alone would only prove the parser agrees with its past self.
- **The text check misses text cut off at the frame edge.** Flag any visible text whose box runs past the left or right edge of the frame (x0 < 0 or x1 > W), anywhere on screen. A line that does not wrap is cut off at both edges, and the safe-zone check reports it only where it crosses the right-hand strip in the lower half.
- **Transcribe the final mix and diff it against the script.** Run speech recognition on the final mix and compare it word by word with the approved script to catch dropped words, mispronunciations, and timing slips.
- **Use Whisper on the final mix, compared with its mishearings of the raw.** Transcribe the final mix with Whisper to catch masking, and compare it with Whisper's transcript of the raw narration. Never change correct captions to match Whisper.

## Delivery

- **Cut a version per platform and length.** Plan each piece as a set of cuts: a 16:9 master, a 9:16 Reels cut of 20 to 45 s, a 4:5 or 1:1 Facebook cut of 60 to 120 s, a 2 to 5 minute YouTube explainer plus a Short, and X cuts of 2:20 or less. Upload at exactly the platform size with the highest-quality setting on.
- **Deliver editable source, not just a video.** Keep the code, text, and timing in a versioned repo so staff can change copy or timing and re-render. Editable source answers the 'one-shot needs adjustments' critique.
- **Deliver poster and last-frame PNGs and never start on an empty frame.** Never start the video on an empty frame. Deliver a poster PNG (about 3 s in) and a last-frame PNG, and hold the last frame at the end.
- **Give timestamped, specific feedback and keep it in the project.** Give feedback as time-coded, specific notes ('at 00:03 hold the product screen longer'), including notes on audio. Keep the storyboard, assets, and feedback in the project folder so every revision uses the same context.
- **Make a phone preview under 30 MB.** Produce a phone preview under 30 MB (H.264 CRF 23, AAC 160k, faststart) for review on a phone and remote delivery, and keep the full master separately.
- **Name deliverables from a manifest.** Name files '<Event or campaign> - <Title> - <FINAL or WITH x> <WxH>.mp4', never reuse a name for different content, and let tools read a manifest instead of parsing file names.
- **Put stills on the review page as short MP4 renders so one page carries the whole review.** The private review page (D-038) plays video renders and takes notes by timestamp; it shows no images. Turn a style frame into a 5 s held still and a tall contact sheet into a slow 24 s pan (ffmpeg loop plus an animated crop), upload them as renders beside the motion test, and the morning approval happens on one page with one set of notes.
- **Record the master's size and SHA-256, and check every delivered copy against it.** At release, write the master's byte size and SHA-256 into a release record, and check that every delivered copy has the same hash. A file name does not prove which render went out.
- **Require a self-review report with every delivery.** Have Claude deliver the MP4 path, a scene table, a short summary of creative decisions, the changes it made after frame review, what is still rough, what it could not verify (such as 'the mix was not listened to'), and NOTES.md.
- **Send a phone preview under 30 MB with every master.** Encode a phone preview under 30 MB (CRF 23, AAC 160k, faststart) and send it with every master, because larger files only show in the desktop app.
- **Send a venue tech note with every event video.** For any video shown at an event, send a tech note: which version to play, keep the speaker's mic live and on top, roll on cue, hold the last frame, 'fit, not fill', no Google Slides import, PowerPoint set to start automatically with rewind and loop off, a walkthrough test, and a USB copy.
- **Ship a poster frame and a last-frame PNG.** Export a poster (cover) PNG and a last-frame PNG from the finished file, decoded with the same color matrix it was encoded with.
- **The end card goes after the presenter's last frame, on its own.** With the presenter's footage untouched, the locked end card cannot sit over her. Cut to the card after her last frame and hold it four seconds after her last word; fade its elements in over a quarter second on white.
- **Tools that cannot run code get exported drawings.** Riverside, Descript and Canva cannot run the engine. Render every reusable drawing to a transparent PNG at 2x (and a traced SVG) with tools/export_drawings.py, keep them in engine/exports/ with a README and a licence row, and point staff there.

## Accessibility

- **Measure caption contrast against the frames behind it.** Check caption contrast against the actual pixels behind each caption, not against the palette, and fix any chunk below 4.5:1.
- **Ship caption files, a transcript, and a sources file.** Deliver burned-in captions for social plus SRT and VTT files, a transcript, a FACTS file, and a README that lists every sound source and license.
- **Size and contrast text for the room.** For a banquet hall, set body text at 44 px or more at 1080p and keep at least 3:1 contrast for large text and 4.5:1 for small text.
- **Target WCAG 2.1 AA with captions, caption files, and a transcript page.** Target WCAG 2.1 AA: caption all prerecorded audio including meaningful non-speech sounds, ship caption files, and publish a transcript page with every on-screen stat and its source, built from the same data as the animation.
- **Use AI for captions and translation without an AI label.** Use speech recognition for caption timing and AI help for translation without adding an AI disclosure, but keep those uses separate from narration.

## Rights and licences

- **A kit's licence is checked in two places and recorded once.** Refuse a kit with no licence text both in the loader (the page never draws it) and in the install tool (nothing is recorded); the install tool writes the asset row with the licence, the author and the parts so kb.py check and the fact that every asset has a licence stay true. No kit is ever downloaded by the engine: a kit is a folder already on disk.
- **Build tools under the GPL: ask the owner, list them, never ship them.** ffmpeg-static ships a GPL ffmpeg, and the phonemizer bundles eSpeak NG (GPL) though its own licence says Apache-2.0. These are acceptable as build tools that never ship; only their output does. Ask the owner, then list them in the project's licence notes.
- **Copy methods, not code, from unlicensed repos.** Learn from unlicensed or non-commercial repos by restating the method in CFC's words, and copy MIT code only with its notice and the upstream commit recorded. MIT covers code, not characters: never publish Claude's Clawd mascot.
- **Credit CC BY music on the end card.** Put the CC BY credit on the end card and in the README. A courtesy credit is fine for public-domain recordings.
- **Draw red string only between documented, sourced links.** Connection lines (red string, arrows) between people or groups must show a documented link, with the source on screen. A conspiracy-board look for undocumented links is a false-light risk and weakens trust.
- **Embed only fonts licensed for video.** Embed only fonts whose license covers video (SIL OFL), with the license file beside them. Do not render with Avenir Next, Garamond, or DIN from the system until CFC confirms the rights.
- **Follow CFC's music licensing rules.** Use only original code-composed music, public-domain compositions in public-domain recordings, CC0, or CC BY with credit. Never use Pixabay, NC, ND, or SA licenses, or any track whose license text you cannot quote from its own page.
- **Keep advocacy true and never automate hidden-AI messages.** Make every advocacy claim true and sourced, never fake a grassroots movement, and never send automated messages to officials or voters at scale that hide their AI origin.
- **Never endorse or oppose a candidate in any CFC video.** Never script a CFC video, or a video for churches to play, that endorses or opposes a candidate, and make church-safe cuts with no candidates and no criticism of officeholders. The Johnson Amendment still binds CFC.
- **Never fake a real screen, document, or record.** Show only real screenshots or generic interface elements; never mock up a real app, bill page, document, or record.
- **Never put an eye bar or 'redacted' treatment on a real person.** Eye bars, blurs, and 'redacted' stamps on real people, officials, or candidates imply guilt and, on an AI likeness, break the no-synthetic-faces rule. Use them only on code-drawn anonymous silhouettes, to mean 'identity protected'.
- **Never synthesize or alter a real person.** Never create synthetic faces or voices of real people, living or historical, never alter a real person's image or voice, and never give an AI a clergy or real-person persona. A 'This has been manipulated' label does not make it lawful for CFC.
- **No testimonial from someone who did not live it.** A presenter speaks to 'you' as a host or explains; nobody describes results or experiences they did not have. Composites are introduced honestly.
- **Put public-domain Bible text on screen.** Use the BSB (public domain since April 30, 2023) as the English default, or KJV, ASV, or WEBP ('LORD'). For Spanish use Reina-Valera 1909, and RVR1960 only within 500 verses, under half of any book and a quarter of the work, with its notice; verify every translation at its publisher and never copy Bible text bundled inside third-party tools.
- **Read every vendored licence file once: a download can save an error page under the licence name.** After vendoring a library or data set, open its licence file and read the first line: a fetch that misses can write the server's error message under LICENSE.txt and the folder then looks licensed when it is not. Record the real licence text and the source beside the file.
- **Research as nobody: no logged-in browsers, no email in requests.** Run research agents logged out, with a generic User-Agent and no email address in any header, URL, or payload.

## Workflow

- **A project's pinned engine copy must be refreshed before a dry run.** A project folder carries its own copy of the engine (new_project.py); after engine fixes, copy core, tools, templates and docs into the project (rsync) before testing there, or the test runs the old code and reports fixed bugs as still present.
- **A stand-in narration fails the explainer gates by design; the dry run proves the pipeline, not the pace.** A dry run that uses a faster stand-in recording (206 wpm) with the real spec's cue words makes beats hold 10 s or more and trips the pace, word-cap and still-run gates. Read those three failures as the stand-in's, not the engine's; the proof is the clean text check, the clean key, the sync confidence and a mastered mix. Only the real recording at 150 wpm can pass the gates.
- **Before touching a shared checkout, look for commits and file changes from the last few minutes.** An overnight session can still be committing when the morning session starts. Before any git command in a shared checkout, read the last commit's time and the files changed in the last 45 minutes; if another session is live, work in your own worktree on your own branch and leave the merge for when it is done (R-019).
- **Check an outside toolkit report against the knowledge base before building anything from it.** When a research report recommends a stack, map each recommendation to what the engine and the knowledge base already have before adopting any of it. Three of six recommendations already existed, one was split across files, one had already lost two comparisons; only one was a real gap.
- **Check every kill-list reason against the code, as for a backlog row.** A scoping session's kill list needs the same check as its backlog rows: read the killed lesson in full, then confirm each stated reason in the code before anyone approves the list. Ask the approval question over the whole list, not part of it.
- **Commit after every verified item.** Commit after each verified improvement, one item per commit, so the last commit is always a working video. Keep steps small in case a limit stops the session.
- **Deliver options in sets of two or three and read his sort.** Offer two or three distinct versions of the element under review, side by side with a comparison page. Treat his Considering and Rejected sort as the strongest taste signal.
- **Describe videos as specs; keep layouts in beat kinds.** A video described as beats in plain text (video.toml), with facts written as fact:<id>, needs no scene code: layouts live once in beat kinds (beats.js) and every future video reuses them. This is the path to staff-made videos without Claude.
- **Fold every fix back into the guide and the trap list.** After each video, write a short review of what the model did well and badly, and turn each fix into a one-line trap or rule with the check that catches it. Carry the list into every new brief.
- **Freeze and fork before a polish pass.** Before handing a delivered version to another session, tag it in git and clone a fork with no remote. Commit one item at a time, with before and after sheets and a PROGRESS row for each.
- **Freeze the contract, then give each worker one scene file.** Freeze the engine, brief, and storyboard before parallel work. Each worker owns one scene file, keeps private helpers inside it, reports bugs in shared files instead of editing them, and guards every cross-scene handoff with a fallback.
- **HyperFrames Studio app: unusable signed out, full-access agent, self-updating, no licence.** Do not install the HyperFrames Studio desktop app on a CFC Mac. It does nothing until you sign in to HeyGen (banned by L-207), runs your own Claude Code or Codex with 'Full access' by default, updates and restarts itself with no off switch, and its code and licence are not public. Its editor is the same web editor the pinned CLI already serves with `hyperframes preview`; test that instead.
- **HyperFrames editor: timeline moves clips, not animations; its agent tool and Undo can damage a project.** Do not use the HyperFrames editor (the web editor or the Studio app around it) for timing changes on CFC cards. Its timeline drags change only clip start and length, not the GSAP tweens, so beats go blank or pop on. Its agent tool studio_update_animation can rewrite a shared helper and report no change, and its project-wide Undo deletes files written outside the app instead of reverting the bad edit.
- **Keep a progress log and a handoff file.** Keep PROGRESS.md (one row per item: what changed, images, checks, commit) and HANDOFF.md (goal, engine map, locked decisions, traps, commands) so any session can resume cold.
- **Never write in another session's folder; fork instead.** One session owns a project folder. To change a delivered piece, fork it into a new folder and branch, and read the original only.
- **Pin an engine copy inside each project.** Copy the engine into every new project with its version, so a delivered video can always be re-rendered exactly, even after the engine improves.
- **Plan every frame and sound as code.** Plan every frame and every sound as code that CFC runs and owns: Claude has no native video, animation, audio, or music feature. Treat partner plugins in the marketplace as third-party tools under their own terms.
- **Plan for usage-limit stops mid-task.** Expect long runs to hit usage limits: keep work committed, write progress as you go, and keep the session open so Claude Code can wait out a five-hour limit.
- **Prove the spec on the script before anyone records (script_check.py).** A spec can look complete and still stop the one command on the real words: a cue word Whisper never writes, a card over 8 words, a hold under 2.5 s, a cut over its word cap. tools/script_check.py times SCRIPT.md at 150 wpm (and 120 and 165), splices the shorter cut's words and builds every cut without rendering; the recording needs no further building only when it says so for every spec (video.toml and video-host.toml).
- **Review renders on a page where notes attach to timestamps.** When there are many renders to review, give the reviewer one page that lists each new render automatically, lets them pause and type a note at a timestamp, writes each note into FEEDBACK.md, and drops a render from the list once it is approved.
- **Standardize one engine for production.** Let Claude pick any stack for experiments, but standardize one engine for production so staff can learn it and edit it.
- **Study a live session without touching it.** To learn from a project another session is editing, export a committed snapshot (git archive <commit>) instead of reading or copying its working tree.
- **The owner judges by eye and ear, not by report.** Every gate is a file the owner plays or looks at with the session stopped: the voice samples, the sample scene, the draft, the takes. A report supports the choice; it never replaces seeing and hearing it.
- **Turn a batch of notes into numbered, committed fixes.** Expect his notes in one batch after he watches. Number them, estimate each in minutes, fix each in its own commit, and show a before and after for each.
- **Turn a good first video into a skill, then a series.** After a piece works, package its look, rules, and pipeline as a skill and reuse it for a series, so each episode keeps the same character, texture, and pacing.
- **Turn each piece of feedback into a one-line rule.** Record feedback verbatim, turn each note into a one-line imperative rule in the right place (TASTE.md or a template), and consolidate the list when it passes about 15 new entries.
- **Two sessions drawing the same object under one asset name: the later module silently wins.** When parallel sessions add props, a shared registry key (the scene asset layer) lets the module that loads last overwrite the other with no error, and a golden frame is the only thing that notices. Register a pack under its own prefix, never overwrite a name that exists, and check the asset names of both branches before the merge.
- **Wait on long jobs with notifications, not sleep loops.** Run long renders and scores in the background and wait on their completion notice or a monitor loop. Do not chain sleep commands, and do not poll by hand.
- **Write handoffs with locked decisions, rules, a timed list, commands, and traps.** When work moves to a new session or model, write a handoff with the goal, locked decisions, binding rules, a prioritized list with timestamps, exact commands, paid-for traps, and finish steps. Tell the new session to ask only about locked decisions or taste calls.
- **Write the hand-over file before the account can run out.** A team account hit its usage limit three times in two days. Keep a HANDOFF file in the repo (paste-able first prompt, state of every branch, open decisions, rebuild and render commands, traps) and refresh it at every delivery, on every branch, so a session on any account continues without loss.

## Taste

- **Aim for clarity and truth, not flash.** Judge every scene by whether it makes the argument clearer. Cut tricks that show off the animation library without carrying a message.
- **Carry a Banned list against Claude's house style.** Put a Banned list in every brief: orange-dot-on-black openers, HUD corner labels, cream editorial pages with italic accent words, '01/02/03' labels, monospace captions, collage paper rips, constant wiggle, particle bursts, decorative glows, template chrome, and false mid-count numbers.
- **Make fewer, excellent, well-sourced pieces.** Invest in a few excellent, well-sourced flagships rather than many average pieces. Name the expert reviewers, state any measurement convention on screen, and finish the source sheet before release so the expected accuracy attack has an answer.
- **No decorative glows or particle bursts.** Do not add decorative glows, halos, or particle bursts. Use light only where the story motivates it: the sun, stadium lights, or light breaking through.
- **Only the door glows: light points the story at one place.** On a night building, light only the opening the story is about (the door, the seal), never every window. A field of lit windows reads as decoration and splits the eye.
- **Review for AI tells before every delivery.** Before every delivery, ask of each frame and sound: would a viewer think AI made this? Remove synthetic voices, synthesized melody, stick figures, glows, count-ups, corner labels, and generic motion first.
- **Use code for text and numbers; real photos for people.** Use code-rendered motion where text, numbers, logos, and timing must be exact, and real photos or footage where faces matter. Do not ask diffusion video for text.
